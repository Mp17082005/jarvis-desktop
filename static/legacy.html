    Help · voice & privacy
    1. Connect to Jarvis, open this page over HTTPS or localhost, and select Enable mic. Choose Allow in the browser microphone prompt. If denied, open this site’s permissions, allow Microphone, then select Enable mic again.
    2. Check Talk mode · speak replies for spoken answers. Select Test voice to try a short greeting, including in text mode. Check your device sound if you hear nothing.
    3. Say “Jarvis” once, or start your request with “Jarvis”. After each answer finishes, keep talking without another wake word. All subsequent speech is treated as conversation until you say stop Jarvis or Jarvis stop listening. These end the conversation, stop playback, and leave the wake detector listening. Use headphones to reduce speaker echo.

    Exact local controls: stop, stop speaking, stop talking, or quiet interrupt even without a wake word, but keep your conversation active. Say Jarvis increase volume, Jarvis decrease volume (10 percentage points), Jarvis set volume to 50 percent, Jarvis mute, Jarvis unmute, Jarvis talk mode, or Jarvis text mode. Other controls need “Jarvis” unless your conversation is active or explicitly recorded with push-to-talk. Ordinary sentences containing “stop” are not interruption commands. Wake aliases include जार्विस, जारविस, జార్విస్, and ஜார்விஸ். Hindi “रुको” and Telugu “ఆపు” interrupt; “जार्विस सुनना बंद करो” and “జార్విస్ వినడం ఆపు” end the conversation.

    During playback or a pending reply, only these local controls are handled; other recognized speech is ignored. Controls act on final recognition results, so interruption speed and simultaneous listening depend on your browser and microphone. Echo can still resemble a control. Stop cancels voice and keeps a pending reply as text; it does not undo server actions. Your next explicit request can speak again. Text mode leaves the enabled mic listening; Stop mic turns listening off.

    Volume controls only Jarvis browser speech, not system volume. Changing active speech volume resumes at the last reported word (that word may repeat); if the browser reports no position, playback stops instead of repeating the entire answer.

    Browser speech recognition may be processed by your browser’s provider. This page forwards only awake requests to your server, never these exact local controls. Push-to-talk uploads each explicit recording for transcription first, then handles local controls without sending them to chat. It cannot listen during playback: Tap Stop speaking to interrupt. Select Record command, speak, then Stop & send; Stop mic discards an unfinished recording.

    Keep this tab open and visible. Background tabs, mobile screen lock, and browser policies can suspend listening. Stopping the mic, hiding the tab, or losing authentication ends the conversation. Returning never enables the mic automatically. Voice replies use your browser’s speech service. Typed chat works without a microphone.

    When permission is required, review the exact tool and arguments, then listen to the complete spoken summary. Only the current action’s exact phrase (for example “approve 4821”) can authorize it by voice after the entire playback finishes. Interrupting the summary leaves approval button-only. Exact action summaries retain message content, including emoji and URLs. Approvals during playback or a pending request are ignored. Generic yes / हाँ / అవును never authorizes a coded action. Say “cancel action”, “no”, “नहीं”, or “వద్దు” to deny the pending action after playback. “stop” only interrupts; it never approves or cancels a server action. If the server supplies no voice phrase, or speech is unavailable, use Approve action or Cancel. Messages, files, and commands are never automatically approved. Public research answers may be spoken without an approval prompt; links remain visible below.
